Transcript

Workshop Facilitator: What business problem are we trying to solve, and who feels it most?

Facilities Lead: Employees report building issues through email, Teams chat, phone calls, and hallway conversations. A facilities coordinator manually rewrites each request, decides how urgent it is, looks for self-service guidance, and then creates a ticket. Employees often do not include the building, floor, exact location, or whether the issue is blocking work.

Help Desk Manager: The help desk receives many of these requests first. We forward them to facilities, but the wording varies. A single issue can create duplicate emails and chats because employees do not know whether someone picked it up.

Workshop Facilitator: What should the future experience look like for an employee?

Facilities Lead: The employee should describe the issue in plain language. The assistant should ask only for missing details: building, location, category, urgency, and a preferred contact method. It should summarize the request back for confirmation.

Workshop Facilitator: What categories matter for the first prototype?

Facilities Lead: Lighting, heating or cooling, plumbing, access or door issues, cleaning, furniture, and safety hazards. Anything involving smoke, exposed wiring, flooding, a blocked emergency exit, or an active security event must be treated as critical and escalated immediately.

Compliance Partner: The assistant must not diagnose electrical, fire, medical, or security conditions. It can provide approved safety guidance and direct the employee to emergency channels, but it cannot say an area is safe.

Workshop Facilitator: Where should answers come from?

Facilities Lead: We have a maintenance FAQ with approved guidance. For example, it can explain how to reset a local thermostat or where to find the after-hours facilities number. If the answer is not in that approved FAQ, the assistant should say it does not know and create or escalate the request.

Workshop Facilitator: What action should the prototype take after confirmation?

Help Desk Manager: For the workshop, it should create a deterministic synthetic service-request receipt with a request ID, category, urgency, summary, and next step. We do not want the workshop to write to the production ticketing system.

Facilities Lead: The live version will eventually create a record in our service-management platform and send a status update. The demo must clearly say the receipt is simulated.

Workshop Facilitator: What should be out of scope?

Compliance Partner: No production credentials, no employee personal data beyond a preferred contact method, no photographs, no building access decisions, no medical emergencies, and no promise of a repair time beyond the approved service levels.

Workshop Facilitator: How will we know the prototype worked?

Facilities Lead: For a common request, the employee should reach a confirmed summary and synthetic receipt in under two minutes. Critical safety phrases should be flagged immediately. The coordinator should not have to rewrite the issue description.

Help Desk Manager: We also want the assistant to handle an unknown building code honestly instead of selecting a different building or inventing a location.

Workshop Facilitator: Give me three example requests we should test.

Facilities Lead: First: 'The lights are out on floor 4 near conference room 402 and a customer meeting starts soon.' Second: 'The kitchen sink on level 2 is leaking slowly.' Third: 'I smell smoke near the west stairwell.' The smoke example should immediately provide emergency guidance and not continue a normal intake flow.

Workshop Facilitator: To confirm: the MVP is an employee-facing facilities assistant that captures and triages a request, answers from approved guidance, and creates a synthetic receipt. The workshop uses only synthetic data and does not touch production. Is that accurate?

Facilities Lead: Yes. Keep the first version focused on those four capabilities.
